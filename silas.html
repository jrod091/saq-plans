Focus Themes

The four things that matter

Lean into what makes you different. Develop what defenders don't expect.

01

Beat down the line with the left

From the LEFT wing, your strongest move is going down the line with your left foot — defenders are trained to push wingers wide and expect a weak-side cross. Yours isn't weak. Make that the defining threat.

02

Cut inside on the right

When you're inverted on the RIGHT wing, the cut inside on your strong left for the shot becomes the lethal version of the Robben/Salah pattern. Practice the in-cut on both flanks.

03

Right foot mastery

Right-foot work runs through the whole session — anchored in ball mastery, then carried into 1v1s, crossing, and finishing. The goal isn't just survival on the right — it's making the defender unable to predict which way you'll go. Two-footed = unmarkable.

04

Striker movement off the ball

When you play through the middle, the move is to find the gap between center-backs, time your run to stay onside, and finish with one touch. Movement creates chances; finishing converts them.

Training rationale

At 11, combined plyometric and change-of-direction training is a strong stimulus for explosive speed and jumping at moderate volume (60-80 foot contacts per session). Your sessions sit in that range — the multidirectional plyos on Mondays are well-suited to your age when mechanics and recovery are handled well.

Reading the defender · decision rules

Beating a defender 1v1 is not a move — it's a read. Memorize these five cues and respond, don't pre-plan. The best wingers in the world all do this without thinking, because they've done it thousands of times.

→ If the defender opens their hips inside · beat them outside, down the line
→ If the defender overplays the line · cut inside on your left
→ If the defender is flat-footed · attack immediately, before they reset
→ If the defender retreats · change of pace and force them to commit
→ If the defender steps hard at you · stop-start or cut back into the space they left
→ If the defender is square / balanced · change speed to unbalance them first
→ If the defender turns their hips early · exploit the opposite side they've committed away from

Position Drill Library

Drills that are uniquely yours

Winger and striker patterns built around your two-footed development arc — left as the weapon, right as the surprise.

LEFT WING · STRONG SIDE

Byline drive + cross

From the left, take the defender on with the left foot down the line, beat to the byline, low driven cross with the left across the box. This is your bread and butter — drill it until it's instinct.
3 sets · 8 reps · alternate finishers
RIGHT WING · INVERTED

Cut-inside finish (left foot)

Start wide on the right. Dribble inside on the diagonal, cut sharply on your left foot at the top of the box, bend the shot into the far corner. Defenders push you inside thinking it's your weak side. Punish them.
3 sets · 6 reps · target far post
RIGHT FOOT · DEVELOPMENT

Right-foot wall block

2 yards from a wall. Right foot only — inside-foot one-touch returns. Count out loud. First miss = restart. Goal: 50 consecutive by week 8 (start around 15).
3 × 30 attempts · log PR weekly
RIGHT FOOT · STRIKING

Right-foot finishing block

From 10 yards, right-foot strikes into a 2yd-wide gate. Inside foot for placement, laces for power — alternate. Build to right-foot strikes after a left-foot setup touch (this is the in-cut from the LEFT wing).
3 × 10 strikes each technique
STRIKER · MOVEMENT

Lose the marker

Stand next to a "defender" (cone or sibling). Server holds the ball at distance. Make a short feint run one way, then sharp move the other way to receive. Train both runs: drop deep to feet, then spin and run beyond.
3 sets · 6 reps each direction
STRIKER · FINISH

First-time finish from crosses

Nora or Isaiah crosses from either flank. One touch finish — no second touch allowed. Side foot to placement, laces if the ball is sitting up. Practice both near-post and far-post arrivals.
3 sets · 6 crosses each flank
