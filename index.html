The Plan

Eight weeks, four phases

Each two-week phase has a clear job. Volume rises through weeks 1-4, intensity peaks in weeks 5-6, and weeks 7-8 detune slightly toward game-realistic, sharp, short work.

PHASE 01

Foundation

WEEKS 1-2

Movement quality before load. Mechanics, mobility, low-intensity ladder and footwork patterns. Reintroduce weak-foot ball mastery. Establish session rhythm.

PHASE 02

Development

WEEKS 3-4

Volume and tempo rise. Harder plyometrics, longer COD sequences, more position-specific reps. Combine ball work with movement load.

PHASE 03

Sharpening

WEEKS 5-6

Reactive decision-making takes over. Visual and auditory triggers. 1v1s under pressure. Synergy patterns become opposed and competitive.

PHASE 04

Peak

WEEKS 7-8

Integration. Short, sharp, game-speed work. Volume drops, intensity stays high, rest increases. Match-realistic scenarios end every session.

Daily Sessions

The 60-minute blueprint

Pick a week, then a day. Every session is built SAQ-first — 8 min warm-up, 16 min athletic development (the engine of the day), 12 min ball mastery, 19 min position synergy, 5 min core/mobility finisher. Weak-foot development isn't confined to the ball-mastery block — it's woven through passing patterns, 1v1s, crossing, finishing, distribution, and the synergy work. Volume, complexity, and decision-load progress across the 8 weeks.

Goalkeeper load · tracked separately

A dive is not a pogo hop

Eli has two separate ceilings — a plyometric contact ceiling (pogos, broad jumps, bounds, hurdle hops) and a separate goalkeeper dive-load ceiling (collapse dives, step-saves, power dives, depth-jump-to-save). Do not combine them into one number. A goalkeeper dive loads the shoulder, wrist, hip, back, and groin in ways a broad jump does not, so it gets its own count. High-amplitude dives, depth-jump-to-save reps, and broad-jump-to-dive reps are treated as very-high-intensity goalkeeper contacts and should remain in single digits per session.

GK dive load — track each separately, both sides logged:
LOW · low dives · collapse dives · set-position resets
MEDIUM · step-saves · reactive step-saves · high-intensity lateral shuffles
HIGH · power dives · distribution volume (drop kicks tax the groin)
VERY HIGH · max-amplitude dives · depth-jump-to-save · broad-jump-to-dive — keep these in single digits, never when fatigued, never stacked

Plyometric intensity classification

Not all contacts are equal — 80 low-level rhythm contacts are nothing like 80 single-leg bounds. Classify every plyo drill into one of these four tiers, and keep the high and very-high tiers deliberately low in volume. The weekly totals above are mostly low and medium tier; the very-high tier should stay in single digits per session.

Low
Jump rope rhythm, small pogos, low mini-hurdle rhythm bounces
Medium
Broad jump stick, double-leg hops, controlled lateral bounds, vertical jumps for height
High
Single-leg bounds, repeated lateral bounds, higher-intensity hurdle hops, alternate-leg bounds
Very high
Depth jumps, depth-jump-to-cut, depth-jump-to-save, broad-jump-to-dive, max-amplitude GK plyo-dive work — keep volume very low, gate by readiness, never when fatigued
What counts as a plyo contact
  • Pogo hops (low)
  • Broad jumps — each landing (medium)
  • Lateral bounds (medium-high)
  • Single-leg hops & bounds (high)
  • Mini-hurdle hops (low-high by intensity)
  • Depth jumps (very high)
  • Horizontal bounds (high)

  • Depth-jump-to-save reps — Eli (very high)

  • Ladder steps (running pattern, not hopping)
  • Sprint reps without jumps
  • Decel sticks (force absorption, no take-off)

Readiness gates · before depth jumps unlock

Isaiah and Eli are scheduled to add depth jumps in week 4. That progression only unlocks if every item below is true on the morning of the session. If any gate fails, regress the drill that day and reassess next session.

Pre-depth-jump checklist

Land cleanly before you fall hard

  • Broad jump landing is quiet — no audible slap, no double-bounce
  • Knees track over toes, no inward collapse on landing
  • Single-leg landings from a 6-inch step are stable for 2 seconds
  • Deceleration sticks (Thursday block) executed without trunk lean
  • No current knee, hip, heel, or back pain — even mild
  • Posture stays tall by the third set of any Monday block (no fatigue collapse)

Goalkeeper safety rules · Eli

Diving and plyometric-to-dive combinations are some of the highest-stress patterns in this program. These rules are non-negotiable for the duration of the 8 weeks and beyond.

Diving safety

Protect the body for the long arc

  • Diving only on safe grass, turf, or padded mat — never on hard ground
  • Stop immediately if pain appears in wrist, shoulder, hip, back, or groin
  • No max-amplitude dives when fatigued — regress to step-save or collapse dive
  • Both sides must be trained evenly across the week — log left/right reps
  • Diving and depth-jump-to-save days are never stacked — at least 48 hours between them
  • Distribution volume monitored — if hip or groin feel tight, reduce drop-kick reps that day
  • Broad-jump-to-dive only when landing mechanics are clean and the surface is safe
Diving progression · 5 stages

Earn each stage before the next

  • Stage 1 · Landing mechanics — collapse dive from set, fall onto side of leg, no max amplitude
  • Stage 2 · Step-save — one explosive step + dive, ball at moderate pace, both sides equally
  • Stage 3 · Low-amplitude power dive — two steps + jump, ball thrown firmly, ground first not hip-first
  • Stage 4 · Reactive step-save — server adds late direction call, dive in response, not in plan
  • Stage 5 · Depth-drop / depth-jump to save — only when stages 1-4 are clean, only on safe surface, only in W4+
Off the Field

The other 23 hours

The on-field session stays capped at 60 minutes — that doesn't change. But the hour of training is only as good as the 23 hours around it. These two pillars are where a lot of the actual development happens: how they recover, and how they learn to read the game. Both are tuned to these four specific kids — not a generic elite template.

01

Recovery culture

A development block is a stress-and-adapt cycle: the training is the stress, recovery is where the adaptation actually happens. Skip the recovery and you're just accumulating fatigue. The single highest-leverage item here is also the cheapest — sleep.

Sleep

The biggest lever, and it's free

Nothing else in this program returns as much as consistent sleep. Growth, motor learning, and CNS recovery all happen here. Keep wake/sleep times consistent — even on weekends — and wind down off screens before bed.

Nora & Silas · ~9-12 hrs
Isaiah & Eli · ~8-10 hrs

Refuel

Real food, soon after

After every session — especially the hard CNS days (Mon, Thu) — eat a proper snack or meal with both carbs and protein within an hour. Keep it simple and food-first: a sandwich, eggs and toast, fruit and yogurt, chocolate milk, rice and chicken. Hydrate through the day, not just at training. No counting, no rules — just refuel well and regularly.

Soft tissue & mobility

10 min on the heavy days

Light foam rolling and easy mobility on Monday and Thursday evenings (the heaviest CNS days) helps the next day feel better. Wednesday's session already serves as active recovery. Eli's daily 10-minute mobility block already covers his — the others can borrow the same routine.

02

Game IQ & film

The on-field plan is strong on micro-tactics — 1v1 reads, defender hips, movement to lose a marker. The macro layer (shape, spacing, when to press) is best added off the field through film. Keep it short and age-tiered, and build it on their own game footage first — that's the most transferable. Roughly once a week, on a rest evening.

Nora & Silas · 15-20 min

Principles, not systems

At 10-11, skip 11v11 systems — that's premature. Watch their own game clips and look for simple principles: creating width, getting depth, supporting angles, and the decision of when to dribble vs. release. Small-group (2v2, 3v3) shapes, not full-team structure.

Isaiah · 20-30 min

Role-specific

Old enough for deeper context. Own-game film focused on his roles: striker movement and timing of runs, CMF scanning and receiving between lines, and the beginnings of when-to-press as part of a unit. Supplement with a pro striker/8 he likes.

Eli · 30 min

Highest-leverage of the four

At ECNL RL + Varsity, film is genuinely high-value for a keeper. Study positioning and angles, starting position vs. through-balls, organizing and commanding the back line, cross management, and distribution decisions. Own-game footage plus a pro keeper to model.
